# Eduraa Mobile: Class Apart

Create experiences users want to return to, recommend, and trust. Do not merely make screens clean or functional. Give Eduraa a recognizable point of view: intelligent, warm, ambitious, calm, and made for students.

## North Star

Make every interaction feel:

- immediately understandable;
- emotionally encouraging without becoming childish;
- premium without becoming cold or decorative;
- fast, responsive, and reassuring;
- unmistakably part of the same Eduraa product;
- worthy of a leading 2026 consumer app.

Design for the feeling: **"This understands me, makes studying feel possible, and is beautifully made."**

Do not copy another product. Match the clarity, craft, responsiveness, and confidence of the best consumer apps while building Eduraa's own identity.

## Product Personality

Express these qualities through every screen:

- **Intelligent:** anticipate the next question and remove unnecessary decisions.
- **Warm:** use humane language, warm surfaces, and supportive recovery.
- **Focused:** make one next action obvious; quiet everything else.
- **Ambitious:** help students feel momentum and possibility.
- **Trustworthy:** never fake data, progress, urgency, or AI certainty.
- **Alive:** use purposeful color, motion, and feedback without visual noise.

Avoid generic SaaS layouts, school-project UI, cold admin dashboards, card grids without meaning, oversized marketing heroes, fake gamification, and decoration that competes with learning.

## Eduraa Signature Language

Use this palette as the stable foundation:

- warm canvas: `#FBF6EC`, `#FFFAF2`, `#FFF7ED`;
- trusted navy: `#07152D`, `#0F172A`, `#111827`;
- Eduraa orange: `#F36C21`, `#F97316`, `#C2410C`;
- primary ink: `#101828`;
- secondary ink: `#667085`;
- dark-surface secondary: `#AAB5C6`;
- elevated surface: `#FFFFFF`;
- warm border: `#E0D6C8`.

Let cream create warmth, navy create trust, and orange create energy. Orange punctuates; it does not flood. Reserve green for real success. Use other colors only when they communicate a clear learning or semantic state.

Use the real Eduraa logo asset. Never substitute a generic letter, improvised mark, or plain wordmark when the asset exists.

Build recognizable signatures across the app:

- warm editorial surfaces paired with confident navy structure;
- concise, student-friendly copy;
- generous but efficient spacing;
- strong typography and alignment before shadows or ornament;
- subtle orange moments that guide attention;
- transitions and feedback that make the product feel responsive and alive.

Do not force every screen into the same composition. Consistency means shared DNA, not cloned layouts.

## AI-Era Visual Energy

Do not confuse minimalism with pale, flat, or unfinished design. A class-apart Eduraa screen must have visual energy and a memorable composition while remaining easy to use.

- Create a deliberate contrast rhythm: dark and light surfaces, quiet and energetic moments, dense and open areas.
- Use organic geometry, controlled asymmetry, layered depth, ambient color, curved paths, intelligent diagrams, or spatial transitions when they strengthen the product idea.
- Avoid endless straight vertical stacks, identical white cards, uniform spacing, and cream-only screens.
- Give each important screen one distinctive visual anchor such as a dark focus surface, intelligent path, contextual illustration, living progress object, or expressive data visualization.
- Keep futuristic treatments warm and credible. Avoid generic neon, glassmorphism everywhere, sci-fi dashboards, glowing robot brains, and meaningless AI sparkles.
- Make AI visible through useful behavior: personalization, anticipation, contextual assistance, adaptive states, and explanations. Do not rely on an `AI` badge to create innovation.

Use original imagery and icons when they improve understanding or emotion:

- Prefer the real Eduraa logo, custom code-native illustrations, coherent icon families, and purposeful learning imagery.
- Generate or commission original bitmap artwork when a photo, character, texture, or editorial illustration materially improves the experience.
- Use photography only when it adds human connection, context, or aspiration; avoid generic stock students and decorative portraits.
- Keep image crops, color grading, corner language, and lighting consistent with Eduraa.
- Provide accessible alternatives and graceful loading/failure behavior for non-decorative images.
- Never add an image merely to fill empty space. Empty space should be intentional; imagery should carry meaning.

## Loveability Standard

For each screen, deliberately create at least one meaningful "love moment." Examples:

- an empty state that gives the student a hopeful, useful next step;
- progress feedback that makes effort feel visible;
- a transition that clearly connects cause and effect;
- copy that reduces anxiety at the exact moment it appears;
- a personalized insight based on real data;
- a recovery path that preserves the user's work;
- a small moment of delight after genuine achievement.

The love moment must improve comprehension, confidence, motivation, or recovery. Do not add confetti, streaks, badges, or animation merely to appear delightful.

## Experience Rules

1. Lead with the user's next meaningful action.
2. Give each screen one dominant purpose and one primary CTA.
3. Prefer hierarchy and whitespace over containers.
4. Avoid cards inside cards and repetitive section boxes.
5. Keep important content and the next action visible early.
6. Use progressive disclosure for conditional or advanced choices.
7. Preserve entered work when navigating, retrying, or switching safe options.
8. Make loading feel intentional and errors feel recoverable.
9. Write short, human, student-friendly copy.
10. Remove anything that does not aid action, understanding, trust, or emotion.

## Mobile Craft

Design and verify for small Android phones, larger phones, safe areas, keyboard-open layouts, long text, increased font size, and slow networks.

Require:

- at least 44x44 touch targets;
- stable layouts without jumps, overlap, or clipping;
- natural scrolling and keyboard avoidance;
- correct input keyboard, autocomplete, capitalization, and return actions;
- visible focus, pressed, selected, disabled, loading, success, and error states;
- inline validation with specific recovery guidance;
- duplicate-submission protection;
- accessible labels, contrast, reading order, and reduced-motion behavior;
- preserved content above bottom navigation and system insets.

Never use popup alerts as the primary response to routine form validation.

## Auth and Registration Standard

Treat login, password recovery, registration choice, registration forms, verification, and onboarding as one continuous emotional journey.

- Keep the real logo and identity treatment consistent.
- Make registration feel like the natural next chapter of login.
- Use a compact header and short supporting copy.
- Present `Institution workspace` and `Individual learner` as refined, immediately understandable choices.
- Keep individual registration as one continuous stacked form unless genuine workflow boundaries require steps.
- Stack names vertically on phone widths.
- Keep role selection compact and unmistakable.
- Reveal institution-dependent fields progressively and explain disabled dependencies.
- Show password guidance progressively.
- Use inline errors and preserve valid inputs after failure.
- Use a trusted navy primary CTA for account creation; use orange for energy, selection, icons, and accents.
- Prevent duplicate creation and clearly explain verification or approval expectations.

## Working Method

Do not begin with mandatory "User Problem," "Root Cause," or other ceremonial status templates. Communicate naturally and briefly.

Before implementation:

1. Inspect the live journey, current code, design tokens, shared components, navigation, and APIs.
2. Compare adjacent screens and identify the product's established visual DNA.
3. State the intended experience and the few decisions that materially shape it.
4. Create a realistic 390x844 visual direction first when composition is uncertain.
5. Obtain approval before implementing a materially new visual direction.

During implementation:

1. Reuse sound architecture, behavior, tokens, and components.
2. Refine or replace a shared component only when it cannot express the intended quality safely.
3. Preserve existing API behavior, navigation, validation, and user data.
4. Implement the whole state model: initial, loading, partial, empty, error, offline, success, and retry where relevant.
5. Keep changes focused, reviewable, and free of fake behavior.

## Visual Proof Loop

Do not rate a UI from source code alone.

1. Render the actual screen on an Android-sized runtime.
2. Capture the initial viewport, keyboard-open state, and scrolled state.
3. Exercise conditional, loading, empty, error, disabled, and success states.
4. Compare directly with adjacent approved Eduraa screens.
5. Critique hierarchy, density, alignment, copy, contrast, touch targets, and emotional feel.
6. Fix visible shortcomings and repeat until the experience holds together.

If live-device inspection is unavailable, say so. Never claim a visual quality score without visual proof.

## Class-Apart Gate

Reject the result and iterate if any answer is no:

- Is the next action obvious within three seconds?
- Does the screen feel unmistakably Eduraa?
- Is there a meaningful reason for every visible element?
- Does it feel warm, intelligent, and ambitious rather than generic?
- Does it have enough contrast, depth, and visual energy to avoid feeling pale or flat?
- Is the composition recognizably current for the AI era without using generic futuristic decoration?
- Is there one genuine love moment?
- Does the experience reduce student effort or anxiety?
- Does it survive small screens, keyboard use, slow data, errors, and retries?
- Is it consistent with adjacent screens without being a clone?
- Would users describe it as beautiful, easy, and trustworthy?
- Would a serious product team confidently publish it in 2026?

Do not stop at "works" or "looks modern." Stop when the experience is coherent, memorable, resilient, and lovable.

## Completion

Report concisely:

- what materially changed;
- the user experience improvement;
- files changed;
- automated checks actually run;
- device and visual states actually inspected;
- edge cases handled;
- remaining risks and exact manual checks.

Never claim testing or visual verification that was not performed.
